# v0.24.0 — gentle daily practice: ASH-style letter, sunlight, reflection, worry postponement

**Date**: 2026-08-10
**Status**: drafted
**Decisions in scope**: ASH-style AI-generated daily letter (reusing the v0.23.0 Phi-4 mini), sunlight reminder, daily 3-question reflection, worry postponement
**Out of scope**: AI coaching chat, voice journaling, mood wheels, habit streaks, sleep diary entry (subjective), letter-from-past-self (user-written), body scan, ACT values clarification

## The four features

| # | Feature | When it shows | Evidence |
|---|---------|--------------|----------|
| 1 | **ASH-style daily letter** | Once a day, at user-chosen time, as a notification | On-device LLM, mirrors Slingshot ASH pattern |
| 2 | **Sunlight reminder** | Once a day, in the first hour of waking | Pjrek et al. 2020, Blume et al. 2019 (meta-analysis) |

## 1. ASH-style daily letter (the headline feature)

### What it is

A short letter (2-3 short paragraphs, ~200 words), written by the on-device Phi-4 mini, based on the user's own data from the past 7 days. The user gets a notification at a time they choose; tapping opens the app to read the full letter. Past letters live in a "Letters" inbox.

The Slingshot "ASH" reference: a daily AI-generated text message that observes your data and gives one small, specific, gentle suggestion. The launcher version is the same shape, on-device, and grounded in data the launcher already collects (no third-party integration).

### Why on-device and not "online" with a model

The launcher is privacy-first. Sending the user's pulse + EMA + notes + sleep data to a cloud LLM would break the existing "no cloud" stance. Phi-4 mini runs on-device in a background worker — same path as the existing nightly report. The data never leaves the phone.

### What the letter observes

The letter is observational, not evaluative. It looks at:

- WHO-5 pulse scores and trajectory over the past 7 days (rising, falling, steady)
- EMA check-in valence and arousal averages and trajectory
- Notes (counts by topic, recent themes, recency)
- Sleep rhythm (bedtime variability, wake variability)
- Patterns from `PatternFinder` (signal ↔ label correlations from the existing nightly report)
- Compassion moments / small things that help (existing pre-emptive data)

The letter does *not* observe:

- Any clinical category (no "depression", "anxiety", "burnout")
- Any source name (the model is told never to name a study, author, or year — same rule as the existing `Prompting.SYSTEM`)
- Anything that isn't on the device

### System prompt (sketch)

The letter uses a different system prompt from the existing `Prompting.SYSTEM`. The existing prompt is for the nightly report paragraph (3-5 sentences, grounded in the day's counts + research). The letter is conversational, observational, and grounded in 7 days of data.

```
You are writing a short, gentle letter to a person about their own past week.
You are not a therapist, doctor, or coach. You are a mirror that notices
patterns in their own data.

Rules:
- Be observational, not evaluative. Notice, don't judge.
- Never congratulate ("great job!"). Never scold ("you should...").
- Never name a study, an author, or a year.
- Never mention a condition, a diagnosis, a symptom, or a risk.
- Pick 1-2 patterns from the data below and reflect them back in plain
  language. If the data shows something concerning, name it gently and
  suggest one small concrete action the person can take this week.
- 2-3 short paragraphs. Plain words. Write to the person, not about them.
- Do not begin with "I". Begin with the observation.
```

The output reuses `NarrationGuard` (the existing safety filter). All the same rules apply — no forbidden terms, no citations, length bounds.

### Prompt input (sketch)

```
This person's past 7 days:

WHO-5 pulse: 4 days scored, scores 14, 16, 18, 15 (out of 25). Trajectory: steady.

EMA check-ins: 12 check-ins, average valence 3.2/5, average arousal 2.8/5.
  Trajectory: valence falling on work days, rising on rest day.

Notes: 6 notes — 3 about work (2 with the word "tired"), 2 about family,
  1 about sleep. Most recent: "Slept 6 hours, did not feel rested."

Sleep rhythm: bed time 23:30 ± 1:15, wake 06:45 ± 0:45. 2 nights later than
  the typical 22:30 wind-down start.

Patterns from the past 14 days:
- Higher resting heart rate on the days you wrote about work.
- Lower check-in valence the day after a late bed time.

Small things you wrote down help: "two minutes outside", "one glass of
  water before phone".

Letter:
```

### Output (sketch — what the model should produce)

```
Your week had a rhythm I noticed. Tuesday was quiet — fewer check-ins,
no notes. The other days had a small, steady pulse, with energy a touch
lower on the days you wrote about work. The two days you wrote about
family had a higher energy reading.

The late bed times this week were the days with the lower check-in
ratings. The wind-down at 22:30 looked like it was working the rest of
the month. Tonight's quiet hours, if you keep them, are probably worth
more than they cost.
```

Observation, no evaluation, no advice-giving, no congratulations. The model "notices" the pattern; the person decides what to do.

### Where it lives

A new entry on the home screen, on the Reading card group (or its own card). The Reading section already has a "Last night's look" entry that says when a report was last generated. The letter inbox is one tap below.

A new "Letters" inbox (like the existing Digest for held notifications, or History for check-ins):
- List of letters, latest first
- Each row: date + first sentence of the letter
- Tap to read full letter
- Delete letter (single tap, no confirm)
- "Generate now" button (uses today+7 days of data, doesn't wait for the schedule)

### Notification

- Single daily notification at user-chosen time (default: 8:00 AM local)
- Title: "A letter from your week" (no name, no app branding — the user knows what it is)
- Body: first 1-2 lines of the letter
- Tap → opens the Letters inbox with the full letter
- Channels: existing `notifications` package, new "letters" channel
- Off by default; opt-in
- "Pause for 7 days" / "Pause for 30 days" affordances (not a streak counter, just a snooze)

### Privacy

- The letter is generated on-device, in a background worker, using the Phi-4 mini already on the phone.
- The notification preview is the first 1-2 lines of the letter, on the lock screen if the user has notifications enabled there.
- The full letter is in the app's local storage, never sent.
- WebDAV backup (v0.23.0) can include letters as a section in the existing `BackupCodec.Backup` data class, behind the same opt-in switch. Cloud sees only the encrypted wrapper.

### Acceptance

- A user with Phi-4 mini installed gets a daily letter at the chosen time. The letter references 1-2 patterns from the past 7 days. The letter does not contain a forbidden term, a citation, or a clinical category — `NarrationGuard` rejects anything that does.
- A user without Phi-4 mini gets no letter, no notification, no empty inbox. The "Letters" entry on the home card is hidden. Same gating as the existing nightly paragraph.
- The same input on two different days produces different output (the LLM is non-deterministic by design — but the same input on the same day produces the same letter, because `LlamaNarrator.seedFor(prompt)` is a stable seed derived from the prompt). The night report and the morning letter can be the same model and same engine, but the letters are their own output stream.
- A user enabling daily letters sees a one-time explainer screen: "Letters are written on this phone by the same model that writes the nightly report. Nothing about you is sent anywhere. You can turn this off, change the time, or delete any letter at any time."
- Finding tests pin: the system prompt forbids clinical terms and citations, the data input is local-only, the `NarrationGuard` runs on the output, the Letters inbox renders oldest-first / newest-first, the daily notification respects the user's chosen time and pauses correctly.

## 2. Sunlight reminder

### What it is

A single notification in the first hour after the user wakes: "Step outside for 10 minutes of daylight." Off by default. Opt-in.

### Why it matters for this user

Shift work, on-call rotations, and the standard 06:00-22:00 phone window of the launcher all stack up against a regular morning-light exposure pattern. The most-cited circadian intervention is morning bright light (10-30 minutes). The 5-HT effect is robust: meta-analyses (Pjrek et al. 2020, Blume et al. 2019) show consistent antidepressant effect for bright-light therapy. The reminder is the smallest possible nudge toward the same mechanism.

### UX

- Toggle in `Settings → Measuring → Wellness signals` (or a new "Daily habits" section)
- Off by default
- On enable: choose the wake time (default: matches the chronotype-derived wake window; or the user picks). The launcher uses the existing `BedtimeList` / wake-time data when available, falls back to a user-set time otherwise.
- Notification text: a small, fixed message — "Step outside for 10 minutes of daylight. The morning light is the strongest signal your body has for the day ahead." (Tone matches the launcher's observational style — no motivation, no streak.)
- Single notification per day. Not recurring on its own. Snooze / skip with one tap.
- Off if the user is in a quiet-hours window (the launcher already knows the user's quiet hours).

### Acceptance

- The toggle is off by default. The notification does not fire unless the user has turned it on.
- The notification fires once per day, in the window the user chose, on days the user is not in quiet hours.
- Snooze / dismiss is a one-tap action that doesn't require opening the app.
- The wake time auto-fills from chronotype data when the user enables the toggle; the user can override.

## 3. Daily 3-question reflection

### What it is

A new home card. Three free-text fields, one tap to expand, one tap to save. Saved as a single "reflection" record per day, distinct from notes (which are open and freeform) and the WHO-5 pulse (which is the 14-day check-in).

The three questions:
- "What was one thing that went well today?"
- "What was one thing that was hard today?"
- "What is one thing you want to remember?"

The fields are independent; a user can fill in one, two, or all three. Empty fields are stored as empty strings (no skipping). The launcher shows a small dot or check when a reflection exists for today, no completion pressure.

### Why three questions

Three is the smallest number that produces a useful reflection without becoming a chore:
- One thing that went well: positive psych, three good things (Seligman).
- One thing that was hard: CBT thought record, naming without judging.
- One thing to remember: anchor, future-self.

The order is fixed (well → hard → remember) so a user builds a habit. A user can answer them in any order; the launcher only stores the final state.

### UX

- New home card: "Today's reflection" with three small fields
- Each field is a single line; tap to expand
- "Save reflection" button at the bottom — saves all three (or all that are filled)
- A user opening the card the same day twice sees the same fields with what they wrote
- A user opening the card a new day sees empty fields for the new day
- A "Read past reflections" entry below — same as the existing Digest / History pattern; shows reflections by date, latest first

### Storage

A new `Reflection` data class with three `String` fields and a `LocalDate`. Stored in a new `ReflectionStore` (mirrors the existing `MomentStore` pattern — DataStore-backed, no schema migration needed because it's greenfield).

### Acceptance

- Three fields, fixed questions, fixed order. Each field is a `String`. Empty is allowed; partial-fill is allowed.
- One reflection per day, enforced by `LocalDate` primary key in the store.
- The home card is silent (no card at all) on days the user has no reflection yet, or shows the existing one.
- Finding tests pin the field order, the per-day uniqueness, the storage shape, and the empty-allowed behavior.

## 4. Worry postponement

### What it is

A new "Worry" entry on the home card and in the notes feature. Type a worry; pick when to think about it (default: today's "worry time" — a daily 15-minute window, default 6 PM). The launcher delivers a notification at the scheduled time: "It's worry time. Open to review."

### How it works

- New "worry" tagged note in `NotesPrefs` (the existing notes store)
- The tag is just a `String` field on the note body prefix (`[worry:2026-08-10T18:00]...`) — the launcher parses the prefix to find worries. Same pattern as the existing `[worry_time]` style markers elsewhere.
- At the scheduled time, the launcher fires a notification from the WorryScheduler (a new WorkManager job, similar to `EmaScheduler`)
- The notification opens the existing Notes screen, filtered to show only worries
- Tapping a worry in the list marks it "thought-about" (the launcher rewrites the note prefix to `[worry_thought:2026-08-10T18:30]...`)
- A user can schedule a worry for any future time ("next week", "tomorrow at 9", "after the meeting")
- A worry that has been thought-about stays in the list with a checkmark; a user can re-schedule it

### Why this works

Worry postponement is a well-supported CBT technique. The mechanism: engaging with a worry in the moment strengthens the worry circuit; deferring the engagement (with a plan to think about it later) reduces the rumination. The "later" has to be a real, scheduled time, not vague.

### UX

- A worry is a note, so the existing notes flow handles it
- New entry on the home card: "+ Add a worry" — opens a small inline editor (worry body, schedule time)
- The notes screen shows a "Worries" section above pinned notes, with the scheduled time and a "thought about" check
- The home card shows the next worry time, e.g. "Worry time: today at 6 PM, 1 worry waiting"
- One notification per worry time, not per worry
- Snooze / re-schedule from the notification or from the worries list

### Acceptance

- Adding a worry creates a note with the `[worry:ISO_TIME]` prefix; the note body is the worry text.
- The WorryScheduler fires at the user's worry time, posts a notification.
- A thought-about worry has its prefix rewritten to `[worry_thought:ISO_TIME]`.
- A worry that passes its scheduled time without being thought-about stays in the list with a "missed" indicator — no streak penalty, just a label.
- Finding tests pin: the prefix format, the schedule-time field, the re-schedule flow.

## What's intentionally not in v0.24.0

- **Letter-from-past-self (user-written).** Distinct from the AI letter; the user didn't ask for it. Future conversation.
- **Body scan.** MBSR technique, useful, but heavier UI work (3-5 min guided audio). Future conversation.
- **ACT values clarification.** Distinct from goals; good for ACT work. Future conversation.
- **Sleep diary entry (subjective).** Existing sleep rhythm is auto-detected; subjective rating is a different signal. Future conversation.
- **Voice journaling.** Needs MediaRecorder, file storage, edge cases. Future conversation.
- **Mood wheels / 8-emotion dials.** Pretty but weak evidence. Future conversation.
- **AI coaching chat.** The letter is the AI touch — chat is too open-ended and the launcher is not a therapist. Future conversation, possibly never.

## Why these four together

- They share infrastructure: the letter uses the LLM, the reflection uses the note-store shape, the worry uses the note-store + WorkManager, the sunlight uses WorkManager.
- They share a release theme: "the gentle, opt-in, daily-practice features" — three or four small things that the user can adopt over weeks without changing how they use the launcher.
- They share the design discipline: nothing counts, nothing streaks, nothing evaluates, nothing congratulates. The launcher stays descriptive, not prescriptive.
- They share the privacy stance: all phone-local, all under the existing data-stores, all covered by the v0.23.0 WebDAV backup if the user opts in.
