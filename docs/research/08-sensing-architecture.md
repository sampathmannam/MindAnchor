# 08: The Sensing Architecture

> How MindAnchor goes from a launcher that senses almost nothing to a
> system that learns one person's own mental-health signature from their
> phone and their wearable — offline, on-device, and without assuming
> anything about them it has not been taught.
>
> This is a design record written before the build, so that the decisions
> can be argued with rather than reverse-engineered later.

---

## 1. What changed

Every previous version of this app deliberately sensed as little as
possible. `docs/research/07` records the reasoning: cross-person inference
from phone signals does not transfer, so the app counted what it could
check and named nothing.

That reasoning was correct **for a product shipped to strangers**. It is
the wrong reasoning for a single person instrumenting themselves.

The pivot is not "we changed our minds about the risk". It is that the
risk was always specifically about *generalisation*, and generalisation is
the one thing an N-of-1 system never has to do.

### The finding that drives everything

Müller, Harari et al. (*Scientific Reports* 2021): a GPS-mobility
depression pipeline scored **AUC 0.82 in N=57** and **0.57 — chance — in
N=5,262**. The 2025 *JMIR* review of mobile sensing for depression: only 1
study in 9 could distinguish worsening from relapse from recovery.

The field read this as "sensing does not work". The more precise reading
is that **population models applied to individuals do not work**. A model
fitted to one person's own history and their own reported states is a
different object entirely, and it is the object the mindLAMP relapse work
found does generalise — within-person anomaly detection against a personal
baseline.

So the design constraint from the app's owner — *"assume, but those should
be based on my data generated by myself"* — is not a restriction imposed
on the engineering. It is the engineering.

### What the research is and is not allowed to do

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Tell us **what to measure** — HRV, sleep-onset variance, circadian drift, unlock bursts | Tell us **what a measurement means for this person** |
| Supply candidate features | Supply thresholds, cut-offs or diagnoses |
| Explain a mechanism once a personal association is found | Assert a mechanism before one is found |

Meaning comes only from the mapping between this person's signals and
this person's own reported states. Nothing is inferred from a population
and applied to them.

## 2. What can actually be sensed

### 2.1 From the phone (already permitted, mostly already collected)

- Screen on/off, unlock timing and frequency
- App foreground sessions and durations (`UsageStatsManager`, already granted)
- Sleep window estimated from screen quiet (`sleep/SleepMath.kt`, already built)
- Gate outcomes — reaches, pauses met, pauses walked through (`friction/GateLedger.kt`, already built)
- Motion and ambient light
- Charging state and connectivity transitions

### 2.2 From the wearable — and the thing that is not there

**Verified, August 2026: COROS writes only exercise and heart rate to
Health Connect. HRV never leaves the COROS app**, on Android or iOS.
COROS also refuses HRV from external chest straps, and has replaced the
raw HRV Index with a computed "Wellness Check" score.

This matters more than it first sounds, because **HRV cannot be recovered
from heart rate**. RMSSD is defined over beat-to-beat RR intervals;
averaged BPM samples have already discarded the variation that *is* the
signal. It is lost information, not compressed information.

### 2.3 The decision: Health Connect as the only wearable boundary

Integrating with the COROS SDK would tie the system to one watch. The
owner's requirement is explicit — it must survive changing wearables.

So the sole ingestion boundary is **Health Connect**: heart rate, resting
heart rate, sleep sessions, steps, exercise, and HRV RMSSD *if any source
ever provides it*. Whatever writes there is read; nothing in the app knows
which brand produced it. Change watch, and the system carries on.

Health Connect is also entirely on-device, which keeps the no-network
property intact.

### 2.4 The decision: camera PPG for HRV

Three routes existed:

| Route | Data quality | Survives a watch change |
|---|---|---|
| BLE RR from the watch | Good, but broadcast is workout-only and blocked while other accessories are paired | No |
| Chest strap (Polar H10) | Research-grade | No — and the owner will not buy one |
| **Fingertip camera PPG** | Good for short-term morning HRV | **Yes — needs no wearable at all** |

Camera PPG wins on the requirement that actually matters here. A daily
morning measurement is also the standard protocol in the HRV literature
rather than a concession: short-term resting HRV on waking is the accepted
method, not a degraded substitute for continuous capture.

It costs one deliberate action per day. That is the only thing in this
design that does.

## 3. Being taught rather than assuming

A system cannot anticipate anything without a model, and it cannot have a
model of *this person* without labels from this person.

### 3.1 Labels

Cheap, frequent, and never a form. A single tap answering one momentary
question — this is ecological momentary assessment, the standard method,
and the app already has the pieces (`pulse/WhoFive.kt`).

Two tiers:
- **Momentary** — a handful of taps a day during a learning phase,
  tapering sharply once the model has signal.
- **Periodic** — the existing validated instruments (WHO-5) at a slower
  cadence, as an anchor the momentary ratings can be calibrated against.

### 3.2 The personal model

- Per-feature rolling baseline: median and a robust dispersion measure
  (MAD, not standard deviation — one terrible week must not redefine
  "usual").
- Deviation expressed as a robust z-score against *that person's* history.
- Association learned between deviation patterns and their own reported
  states.
- Nothing is reported as meaningful until the association has survived
  enough data to not be noise.

The existing `sleep/Deviation.kt` is the smallest possible version of this
and sets the tone: report the count, name nothing.

### 3.3 The honesty rule

Anticipation is allowed — the owner asked for it explicitly. What is not
allowed is anticipation dressed up as certainty. Every forward-looking
statement carries what it was derived from, so it can be checked and
argued with.

## 4. The model, the corpus, and the nightly report

### 4.1 Constraints

- **No network, ever.** The app holds no `INTERNET` permission and will
  not gain one. Model and corpus arrive via the Storage Access Framework —
  the same route backups already use — copied from a computer once.
- Target device: 12 GB RAM, with an **8 GB floor** to support.
- Generation is background-only. The person never waits.

### 4.2 Choices

**Runtime: llama.cpp.** GGUF means the model can be swapped without
rewriting anything, weights are memory-mapped natively rather than living
on the Java heap, and it supports the widest range of models. MediaPipe's
LLM Inference API has been deprecated in favour of LiteRT-LM, which is
Android-only and narrower.

**Model: 3–4B class at Q4_K_M.** Measured footprints put Gemma 3 4B at
≈2.9 GB, Phi-4 Mini at ≈2.7 GB, Llama 3.2 3B at ≈2.2 GB; 8 GB is the
stated floor for the 3–4B class, which is exactly the device floor.
Leaning **Qwen3.5-4B** for its very long native context — a nightly report
must hold weeks of personal history *and* retrieved passages in one pass,
so context length binds report quality harder than raw capability does.

Throughput will be ~10–13 tokens/sec. Irrelevant: it runs on the charger
while the person sleeps.

**Knowledge: retrieval, not weights.** A phone-sized model cannot hold
this literature in its parameters and will confabulate citations if asked
to try — the worst possible failure for a system whose entire claim is
groundedness. So the corpus lives on-device as indexed text, the model
retrieves actual passages, and every statement can point at the paragraph
it came from. This is what makes "expert in the research" both achievable
and *checkable*, and it enforces the honesty rule mechanically.

### 4.3 When it runs

**Charging, screen off — in practice overnight.** One window satisfies
every constraint simultaneously: nobody is waiting, battery cost is nil on
mains, an idle phone will not thermally throttle, and the report is
finished by morning. Additional runs fire opportunistically whenever the
phone is next on the charger.

### 4.4 What a report contains

1. **What changed** — signals against this person's own baseline, as counts.
2. **Why that might matter** — retrieved from the corpus, source attached.
3. **Something tailored** — an explanation or exercise shaped by what is
   actually true of them this week.

## 5. Build order

| Stage | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pure signal processing: PPG → RR → HRV, with artifact rejection and a quality gate that refuses rather than guesses |
| 2 | Health Connect ingestion, every data type independently optional |
| 3 | Camera capture surface and the daily measurement |
| 4 | Labels — momentary EMA plus the existing periodic instruments |
| 5 | Personal baseline and deviation model |
| 6 | Corpus, index, retrieval |
| 7 | Model import via SAF, nightly generation job, report surface |

Stages 1 and 2 are pure logic and are testable without a device, which is
the pattern that has held all through this repo: the decision lives in a
pure function with tests aimed at the ways it hurts somebody, and the
Android glue around it stays thin enough to read.

## 6. What this design does not solve

- **Camera PPG needs a deliberate daily action.** Everything else here is
  passive; this is not. If it gets skipped, HRV goes missing and the model
  says so rather than guessing.
- **The wearable may contribute little.** If COROS only ever supplies
  heart rate and exercise, most of the physiological signal comes from the
  camera, not the watch.
- **N-of-1 findings are N-of-1.** What is learned here is a method that
  might transfer, not a result that does.
- **The wording still needs a clinician.** Unchanged from `07`. Building
  the mechanism does not resolve it, and it will land hardest on the
  person it is meant to help.
