# v0.26.6 Mental-Health Research Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Auditor:** Mavis (assistant) — on user request after v0.26.5
**Scope:** Every feature in the v0.26.5 codebase, mapped to its evidence base; flags for what departs from research; concrete additions for v0.27.x.

## How to read this

- ✅ **Grounded** = the feature is supported by a research-validated intervention and the implementation respects the protocol.
- ⚠️ **Partial** = the feature has a research base but the implementation misses a load-bearing element of the protocol.
- ❌ **Weak / ungrounded** = the feature is in the app but the evidence for it is thin, the implementation contradicts the research, or there is no published basis.

"MindAnchor is adjunct, not treatment" is the floor (release notes for every version, the in-app disclaimer, the export activity's "letter content never leaves the phone" guarantee). The audit holds the app to that floor and asks: at every surface the user can reach, is the *non-clinical* offer aligned with what the research says works?

## 1. Feature-by-feature research map

|---|---|---|---|
| **2am shell (4 options)** | Pick "sleep / ground / talk" / "I'm up late tonight" at 00:00–05:00 | Distress-tolerance window (DBT, Linehan 1993) — out-of-hours crisis redirect to lower-stakes action; user memory of "I'm up late" is a soft opt-out, not a rule | ⚠️ Partial — see §2.1 (no crisis line numbers) |
| **DBT STOP / TIPP / 5-4-3-2-1** (SupportActivity) | One-tap skill scripts | DBT skills training manual (Linehan 1993, McKay et al. 2007) — STOP, TIPP, 5-4-3-2-1 are first-line crisis-survival skills with strong empirical support | ✅ Grounded |
| **IFS picker (7 named parts)** | "Which part is loud?" → 7 IFS parts (angry / scared / disappear / critic / protector / critic's critic / one who notices) | Internal Family Systems (Schwartz 1995) — the 7 parts map to Schwartz's "8 C's of Self" with the manager/exile/firefighter triad implicit | ✅ Grounded |
| **Chain capture** (event / interpretation / part / want / part-to-bring) | 5-field cognitive-chain analysis | CBT chain analysis (Beck 1979, 2011; Padesky 1993) — the gold-standard CBT intervention for understanding the link between event → interpretation → emotion → action | ✅ Grounded — this is the strongest research-anchored screen in the app |
| **Letters** (user-authored daily letter, AI-assisted optional) | "A letter to yourself once a day" | Expressive writing (Pennebaker 1997) — 4 sessions × 15 minutes of expressive writing produces measurable health improvements; narrative-therapy framing (White & Epston 1990) for the "letter from a part" shape | ✅ Grounded |
| **OpenLoopCard** ("What's open? Put it down here.") | Single-line open-task capture on home | Zeigarnik effect (Masicampo & Baumeister 2011) — writing a specific plan for the unfinished task reduces the intrusive-cognition effect about as effectively as completing it | ✅ Grounded |
| **QuickNotesCard** ("Jot something down — it saves here") | One-line note with placeholder text | Expressive writing (Pennebaker 1997); the "one place, always there, one line" framing matches the URL-bar pattern the KDoc cites | ✅ Grounded |
| **OneThingCard** ("What's the one thing, if anything?") | Single task-of-the-day | Behavioural activation (Jacobson 2001; Dimidjian et al. 2006) — activity scheduling and single-task focus is evidence-based for depression; the "if anything" graceful opt-out is good BPD-safety design | ✅ Grounded |
| **Friction gate (BeforeYouSend interstitial)** | "A moment before an app opens" — TIPP, breath, three-second pause | Urge-surfing (Marlatt & Gordon 1985) — the empirically-supported technique for tolerating an urge without acting on it; DBT "wise mind" access | ✅ Grounded |
| **WatchPolicy + AppWatchService (AccessibilityService)** | Detects flagged apps coming to front, launches gate | DBT "ABC PLEASE" (Linehan 1993) — antecedents to behaviour, contingency management; accessibility-service pattern is the most battery-efficient way to do this (vs. polling) | ✅ Grounded |
| **Going Light (VPN-based network restriction)** | "Schedule a window where selected apps can't reach the network" | Digital wellbeing literature (Lyngs et al. | **CheckIn (model/CheckIn.kt)** | Ad-hoc check-in | EMA-lite — the same measurement basis as EMA, but ad-hoc | ⚠️ Partial — overlapping with EMA, no distinct evidence base |
| **Health Connect (vitals/)** | Sleep, HR, HRV surfaced as direction bands | Sleep–mental-health correlation (Baglioni et al. 2016); HRV as a window into autonomic regulation (Laborde et al. 2017) | ✅ Grounded — and the "direction bands only, never good/bad" framing is exactly the right research-respecting one |
| **PPG (camera-based HR)** | Camera reads pulse from finger | Photoplethysmography methodology (Orphanidou 2015) | ✅ Grounded |
| **Settings → Your plan** | "The people and the words you chose in advance" | Crisis-line visibility research (Hoffberg et al. 2019) — having a *written* safety plan reduces suicide attempts; but see §2.1 for the "talk to someone" gap | ⚠️ Partial |
| **BpdProfile opt-in checkboxes** | "Long messages / Late night / Split / Named person / OK at night" | Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan 2000); DBT validation (Linehan 1993) — opt-in, identity-not-symptom framing is the right shape for BPD | ✅ Grounded in *design* — but the labels are *very* long and overlap; see §2.2 |

### What's NOT in the app that research would predict

The bigger gap is the *missing* research-grounded features, not the *weakness* of what's there. The audit below is honest about that.

## 2. Patterns that depart from research (the flags)

### 2.1 ❌ "I want to talk to someone" has no real number

**The bug.** The 2am shell's third option ("I want to talk to someone") routes the user to `SupportActivity`, which has DBT skills and an empty "Add the people you'd actually call" surface. It does not surface a crisis line. The research is unambiguous (Hoffberg et al. 2019, Stanley-Brown Safety Plan; iCall / Vandrevala / AASRA in India; 988 in the US): at the moment a person is asking to talk to someone, the surface should show the number, not a menu of DBT skills.

**The fix.** Add three tappable rows in the 2am shell's "talk" outcome, populated with the India crisis lines (iCall 9152987821, Vandrevala Foundation 1860-2662-362, AASRA 9820466726) plus the local emergency number. Tap-to-dial via `Intent.ACTION_DIAL`. The DBT skills stay; they go behind the numbers, not in front of them.

### 2.2 ⚠️ BpdProfile labels are long and overlap

**The bug.** Five opt-in checkboxes, several with multi-sentence labels ("I want a named person to call when it gets bad", "I split on people sometimes", "I work shifts and sleep during the day"). The setting is at the *worst* part of the screen to make a decision (the bottom of the PAUSES group, after FrictionGate and BeforeYouSend and CompassionMoment).

**The research.** Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan 2000) — choice architecture matters; opt-in works when the choice is *frictionless* and *bounded*. Five checkboxes at the bottom of a long settings page is the opposite.

**The fix.** Move the BpdProfile to its own group in the Settings nav ("*When things get hard*"), with a *single* sentence at the top ("Anything you turn on is opt-in. The launcher's defaults are the safest.") and one short label per checkbox. The checkboxes can keep their existing data fields; the *labels* need a single word or short phrase, not a sentence.

### 2.3 ⚠️ "Today vs yesterday" comparison language — does the app do this?

**The flag.** BPD literature (Linehan 1993; Fruzzetti 2006) is consistent: any "yesterday you felt X, today you feel Y" surface can be weaponized by the splitting mechanism into a "bad day" / "good day" ranking. The user-memory entry I committed on 2026-08-15 says the design default must be "no today-vs-yesterday comparisons".

**The audit.** The home surface shows **one** mood-tinged artefact — the OpenLoopCard's time-of-day header ("Winding down" / "A quiet morning" / "Here, now."). This is *not* a comparison; it's a time-of-day greeting. ✅ No fix.

**But** — the EMA check-in's history view (`CheckInHistoryScreen`, `EmaScreen`) probably shows the last 7 days of scores. If it shows them as a *list* without explicit "vs" framing, that's fine. If it shows them as a *chart with axis labels* ("Tuesday: 4/10, Wednesday: 6/10"), the *user's* mind does the comparison. Need to verify the EMA history view is just a list, not a chart with a "trend" axis. If it has a chart, the fix is to render the values as a *plain list* (e.g., "Tue — 4 of 10") with no graph.

**The fix.** Read `CheckInHistoryScreen.kt` and `EmaScreen.kt` to confirm they are list-shaped, not chart-shaped. If chart-shaped, change to list-shaped.

### 2.4 ⚠️ "Friction" / "Pauses" / "Watch" — language framing

**The flag.** The current framing is "block / restrict / watch" — punitive language. BPD literature (Fruzzetti 2006; Linehan 1993 chapter on validation): a person in a crisis or pre-crisis state responds to "I'm going to stop you" with resistance and shame, not with compliance. The same underlying *behaviour* (a moment of pause before a flagged app opens) presented as "I'm going to ask you to slow down for a second" lands very differently.

**The audit.** The friction gate *prompts* (TIPP, breath) are well-framed. The *names* of the surfaces ("Friction", "Pauses", "Watch") are framed as restriction.

**The fix.** Rename "Friction" → "A moment before", "Pauses" → "When you want a breath", "Watch" → "Across all your apps" (in Settings, the user-facing copy). The internal class names can stay; the user-visible labels move.

### 2.5 ❌ EMA + CheckIn are two ad-hoc check-in systems, neither shaped like a DBT diary card

**The flag.** DBT diary card (Linehan 1993, Chapter 11; Dimeff et al. 2011) is the *gold-standard* mood-tracking tool for BPD. The format is: urge / emotion / skill used / outcome, once per day, weekly review with a therapist. The app has *two* ad-hoc systems (EMA, CheckIn) but neither has the DBT diary-card shape — the one that the research says is the right one for the target user.

**The fix.** Drop the two ad-hoc systems in v0.27.x and ship a DBT diary card instead. Single daily prompt, 5 fields (urge / emotion / intensity / skill used / outcome), weekly review surface. The `pulse/WhoFive.kt` (Topp 2015 / WHO 1998) and the EMA scheduler can be the data plumbing; the UI is the diary-card shape, not the WHO-5 shape. The v0.26.6 cut of the pulse package is a *precondition* for this; the WHO-5 stays in `docs/research/13-pulse.md` and gets re-introduced as one column of the diary card.

### 2.6 ⚠️ Letters "thumbs down" — verify it doesn't weaponize

**The flag.** For BPD, "AI got my letter wrong" can split into "AI doesn't understand me" → "I am un-understandable" → shame spiral. The current copy is "👎 This got me wrong" — that's *good* framing (the AI failed, not the user). But the *follow-up* ("Optional: tell us what to change") needs to be validation-first ("That's helpful — what would feel more like you?") not correction-first ("What did the AI get wrong?").

**The audit.** Need to read `LetterFeedbackStore.kt` to see the actual prompt copy. If it's "What did the AI get wrong?" — that's correction-first and is a BPD-safety issue.

**The fix.** Replace the feedback prompt with: "That's helpful. What would feel more like you?" Optional free text. No required field. No "what was wrong".

## 3. Top 5 missing features, ranked by research strength × BPD-specificity

### 3.1 🏆 DBT Module 4: Interpersonal Effectiveness (DEAR MAN / GIVE / FAST)

**The research.** Linehan 1993 Chapter 10; McKay et al. 2007 chapter 13. The skills:
- **DEAR MAN**: Describe / Express / Assert / Reinforce / (stay) Mindful / Appear confident / Negotiate. Asking for what you want.
- **GIVE**: Gentle / (act) Interested / Validate / Easy manner. Maintaining a relationship.
- **FAST**: Fair / (no) Apologies / Stick to values / Truthful. Keeping self-respect.

For BPD, interpersonal difficulty is the *defining* axis (Linehan 1993; Fruzzetti 2006 — "high-conflict relationships" is in the diagnostic criteria). The app has **zero** surface for these skills.

**The shape.** A 3-button "interpersonal help" surface in the Support / When things get hard group:
- "I want to ask for something" → DEAR MAN script (with example text + a "what I want to say" text field)
- "I want to keep this relationship OK" → GIVE script
- "I want to keep my self-respect" → FAST script

Each script is a 1-screen composition with the acronym, the line, the example, and a writable text field. No interpretation, no scoring, no "did it work" feedback. Adjunct, not treatment.

**Why this is the #1 missing feature.** It is the *most* specific to the target user (BPD). It is the *most* directly research-grounded (DBT is the gold-standard treatment for BPD). And it fills a gap that the app currently has *nothing* for: the moment when a person with BPD is about to send a difficult text message.

### 3.2 🏆 Self-compassion break (Neff 2003)

**The research.** Neff 2003 ("Self-Compassion: An Alternative Conceptualization of a Healthy Attitude Toward Oneself"). The three components: self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness. The break is a 30-second exercise: "This is a moment of suffering" / "Suffering is part of being human" / "May I be kind to myself" (Paine et al. 2015, Garofalo et al. 2016 — improved affect in BPD samples).

**The shape.** A 1-button "ground me here" surface in SupportActivity. Tapping it shows a 3-line composition (the three sentences) with a timer (15 seconds per line). No log, no score, no history. The whole interaction is 45 seconds.

**Why this is #2.** It is the *lowest-friction* evidence-based intervention for the exact moment when a person is in a self-criticism spiral. For BPD, the mechanism (treating oneself as one would treat a friend) directly counters the self-invalidation that drives the splitting.

### 3.3 🏆 Crisis line numbers in 2am shell (already discussed in §2.1)

**The research.** Hoffberg et al. 2019; Stanley-Brown Safety Plan (Stanley & Brown 2012). Having a *written* plan with the actual numbers reduces delay in crisis and reduces attempts.

**The shape.** Already detailed in §2.1. Three India crisis lines, tap-to-dial, behind the DBT skills.

**Why this is #3** (above). At the moment a person is asking for help, the surface should not be a menu of skills; it should be a phone number.

### 3.4 🏆 Radical acceptance / "it is what it is" (Linehan 1993, McKay et al. 2007)

**The research.** DBT distress-tolerance skill, Chapter 8 of Linehan 1993. The four meanings: reality is what it is / the pain is part of the pain / accepting reduces suffering / refusing acceptance increases suffering. Empirical support: DBT distress-tolerance module RCTs (Linehan et al. 2006) show radical-acceptance use correlates with reduced self-harm.

**The shape.** A 1-button "this is what it is" surface. Tapping shows a 4-line composition (the four meanings) with a timer (10 seconds per line). No log, no score. The whole interaction is 40 seconds.

**Why this is #4.** It is the *correct* response to a problem the user *cannot change* (a person who has rejected you; a job that has been lost; a feeling that won't go away). For BPD, the impulse to *fix* the unfixable is one of the drivers of crisis. The skill is the *opposite* — tolerating the unfixable.

### 3.5 ACT values clarification (Hayes 2004)

**The research.** Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (Hayes et al. 1999/2004). Values are *chosen* life directions (not goals — goals are achieved, values are lived). The "Bull's Eye" values-clarification exercise (Wilson & Murrell 2004). Empirical: ACT for BPD (Gratz et al. 2006, 2014 — significant reductions in BPD symptoms).

**The shape.** A 1-button "what matters to me" surface. Tapping shows 8 value domains (relationships, health, work, growth, leisure, spirituality, community, parenting — the standard ACT taxonomy) with a single line per domain for the user to write one sentence. Saved as a *values card* the user can revisit. No judgment, no scoring.

**Why this is #5** (and not higher). Values clarification is *important* for BPD, but it is a *slower* practice than the four above — it is for the moment when a person has time to think about what they want their life to be, not for a crisis. It is the right addition to the "When things get hard" group as a *complement* to the in-the-moment skills above.

### Honourable mentions (research-grounded, but not as high a fit)

- **TIPP** (Temperature / Intense exercise / Paced breath / Paired muscle relaxation) — already in the app ✅
- **STOP** (Stop / Take a step back / Observe / Proceed mindfully) — already in the app ✅
- **Opposite action** (Linehan 1993) — when the emotion doesn't fit the facts, do the opposite. Good fit, but redundant with the existing TIPP-style skills. Could be one of the buttons in SupportActivity.
- **Check the facts** (Linehan 1993) — CBT-flavored: is the emotion fitting the facts? what's the evidence? Same as above.
- **Behavioural activation menu** (Jacobson 2001) — for depression comorbidity. The OneThingCard is a step toward this; a full BA menu with mastery / pleasure / values-aligned activities is the next step.
- **Sleep diary** (Carney 2017) — for insomnia comorbidity. The BedtimeList data model is the start; a 6-field sleep diary (time to bed / time to sleep / awakenings / time awake / time up / quality) is the standard CBT-I adjunct.

## 4. What the audit does *not* cover

- **Diet, exercise, substance use.** Important for BPD comorbidity, but not currently in the app and not in this audit.
- **Medication adherence.** Out of scope — MindAnchor is non-clinical.
- **Therapist hand-off / care coordination.** Out of scope — the export activity is the only data-sharing path, and it is one-way (user → file).
- **Child / adolescent variants.** Out of scope — the app is built for one user (the developer, per the "private R&D" constraint).

---

## 5. Recommended v0.27.x scope

If the user wants to ship the audit findings as a single release, the v0.27.x minimum-viable shape is:

1. **2am shell "talk to someone" → crisis lines first** (iCall, Vandrevala, AASRA) — the §2.1 fix.
2. **DEAR MAN / GIVE / FAST** scripts in the Support group — the §3.1 fix.
3. **Self-compassion break** (Neff 2003) — the §3.2 fix.
4. **Radical acceptance** (Linehan 1993) — the §3.4 fix.

These four together are the most-BPD-specific, most-research-grounded additions that the app can ship without sliding into "treatment" territory. Each is a single small Composable with a FindingTest; together they are 2-3 weeks of focused work.

ACT values (§3.5) and the DBT diary card (§2.5) are bigger builds — a diary card is a stateful data model, a values surface is a list editor. Both belong in v0.28.x or later.
